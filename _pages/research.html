Research

I study computational methods for approximating maps between functions, particularly solution operators associated with partial differential equations. My work combines approximation theory, numerical analysis, kernel methods, and machine learning.

Kernel approximation and operator learning

I investigate how kernel expansions can represent functions and operators on regular grids, irregular grids, and scattered point sets. A central question is how the choice of kernel basis, centers, polynomial augmentation, and regularization influences accuracy and generalization.

Adaptive Kernel Expansion DeepONets

Ongoing work with Professor Grady B. Wright

Adaptive Kernel Expansion DeepONets explore learned kernel representations inside operator-learning architectures. The project studies accuracy, cross-grid evaluation, and the relationship between classical kernel approximation and learned function representations. TreeAKED is a hierarchical extension designed to organize these representations across levels.

Polyharmonic splines for operator learning

This work studies polyharmonic splines as interpretable, shape-parameter-free kernels for function reconstruction and operator learning. The project connects polynomial augmentation and classical scattered-data approximation with modern learning architectures.

Cross-grid generalization

Many learned operators are trained and tested on one fixed discretization. I study how kernel and neural-operator models behave when evaluation points, grid resolution, or point distributions change between training and inference.

Emerging directions

I am interested in multiscale kernel frames, divergence-free and curl-free approximation, Helmholtz–Hodge decompositions, point-cloud reconstruction, and uncertainty quantification. These directions connect meshfree approximation with physically structured scientific learning.

Computational practice

My research workflow combines mathematical formulation, reproducible implementation, and systematic experiments on local GPUs and high-performance computing systems. Benchmark problems include advection, Burgers’ equation, Darcy flow, Helmholtz problems, structural mechanics, and Navier–Stokes.
