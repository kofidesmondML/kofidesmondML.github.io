About me

I am a PhD student in Computing with a Data Science emphasis at Boise State University, where I work with Professor Grady B. Wright. My research lies at the intersection of computational mathematics, kernel approximation, scientific machine learning, and numerical methods for partial differential equations.

Before Boise State, I earned an MSc in Mathematical Sciences from the African Institute for Mathematical Sciences Rwanda and a BA in Mathematics with Economics from the University of Ghana. I have also worked in machine learning and research software, with experience spanning numerical linear algebra, GPU computing, data analysis, natural language processing, and computer vision.

Research

My work focuses on developing and evaluating computational methods for learning solution operators arising from PDEs. I am especially interested in how classical approximation theory can inform the design of accurate, interpretable, and discretization-flexible learning methods. My interests include

  • kernel approximation and radial basis functions,
  • operator learning and neural operators,
  • scientific machine learning for PDEs,
  • polyharmonic splines and structured kernel bases,
  • multiscale and structure-preserving approximation, and
  • uncertainty quantification for scientific models.

Current projects study adaptive kernel expansions, cross-grid generalization, and kernel-based correction of learned operators. See the Research page for additional details.
